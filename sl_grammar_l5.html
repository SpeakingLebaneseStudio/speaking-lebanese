Time & Calendar
Activity 4
Grammar in Context

Time, day, and "I have" — the lightest lesson in the arc

This lesson doesn't teach new machinery — it puts word order you already know (Lesson 1's zero-copula) and words you already know (Lesson 2's numbers) to a new job: telling time and naming the day. Then two small new words, 3endi ("I have") and fiyi ("I can"), reuse the same possessive endings you learned in Lesson 3.

Telling time in Lebanese follows the same "hour, then minutes" order English does — you just build it with sé3at (hour), noss (half), rob3 (quarter), w (and/past), and illa (to/minus). Days of the week and the calendar words that go with them (yom, jem3at, shahr, séné) round out the lesson, so you can say when something happens, not just what it is.

Rule 1 — Telling Time
addey el sé3at? — el sé3at [number] w / illa [fraction]
el sé3at setté w rob3 → it's a quarter past six
"what time is it?" starts every exchange — the answer just reuses Lesson 2's numbers

"W" (and) adds minutes past the hour; "illa" (minus) counts back from the next hour. Same numbers you already know from Lesson 2, just doing new work:

الساعة وحدة و نصّ
el sé3at we7det w noss
it's half past one
الساعة ستّة و ربع
el sé3at setté w rob3
it's a quarter past six
الساعة حدعش الّا خمسة
el sé3at 7da3ch illa 5amsé
it's five to eleven
Rule 2 — Days & Calendar
ne7na el yom shou? — el a7ad, el tanein, el taléta...
jem3a → el jem3a
week → Friday — every day of the week takes "el," the same zero-copula pattern from Lesson 1

Yom (day), jem3at/ousbou3 (week), shahr (month), and séné (year) are the four building blocks — the days themselves all start with el, same as the nouns you already know:

نحنا اليوم شو؟
ne7na el yom shou?
what day are we?
أدّي بالشهر اليوم؟
addey bel shahr el yom?
what's the date?
اليوم الجمعة
el yom el jem3a
today is Friday
Rule 3 — "I have..." / "I can..."
3endi + noun · fiyi + verb
wa2t → 3endi wa2t
time → I have time — the -i ending is the same "my" suffix from Lesson 3

3endi and fiyi aren't verbs the way English "have" and "can" are — they're built from the same possessive endings you already learned attaching to family words like jiddo or 3ammo in Lesson 3. Once you know one, you know the whole set:

عندي، عندك، عنده، عندا
3endi, 3endak, 3endo, 3enda
I have, you have, he has, she has
فيي روح هلق
fiyi rou7 halla2
I can go now
Rule 4 — "There is / isn't"
fee + noun · ma fee + noun
shams → fee shams
sun → there's sun — fee shares a root with fiyi, but never changes shape

Unlike fiyi, plain fee doesn't take endings — it just sits in front of the noun, most often when you're talking about the weather:

في شمس اليوم
fee shams el yom
it's sunny today (there's sun today)
ما في وقت
ma fee wa2t
there's no time
💡
Put time and possession together — full sentences, not just fragments

Once telling time and 3endi/fiyi are both comfortable, you can chain them into real sentences: 3endi 5amsta3ch d2i2a — "I have fifteen minutes" — or fiyi eeji 3al sé3at setté — "I can come at six o'clock." Nothing new here grammatically — it's Lesson 2's numbers, Lesson 3's possessive endings, and this lesson's time words, just working together.

✓ 3endi 5amsta3ch d2i2a — I have fifteen minutes
✓ fiyi eeji 3al sé3at setté — I can come at six o'clock

Both reuse pieces you already have — the new part is just knowing how to stack them.
