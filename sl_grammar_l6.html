Activity 4
Grammar in Context

Every verb starts with "b" — and that "b" changes shape depending on who's doing it

This lesson's vocabulary bank gave you the "I" form of over 50 verbs — bfee2, bektob, brou7. That's one-eighth of the picture. Lebanese present-tense verbs conjugate across eight persons, and the b- prefix that starts every one of them shifts shape for each.

You already know the eight pronouns from Lesson 1 — ana, inta, inti, houwwé, hiyyé, ne7na, into, hinné. The pattern below attaches to almost every verb you'll ever learn, so it's worth locking in now: this is the single biggest grammar milestone in the whole course.

Rule 1 — The b- prefix conjugates across all 8 persons
b- + person marker + verb stem
bektob → btektob / byektob / mnektob…
I write → you write / he writes / we write — same verb, eight shapes

The full set, using bektob (I write) — one of this lesson's own vocabulary words — as the model:

Every "I" verb in this lesson's vocabulary bank — bfee2, b2oum, bedross, brou7, and the rest — is already the ana row of its own version of this exact grid. Learning the pattern once means you can conjugate all fifty-plus of them.

💡
"btektob" is doing two jobs at once

Look at the grid again: inta (you, m) and hiyyé (she) both take the exact same bt- prefix — btektob written and pronounced identically for "you (m) write" and "she writes." Lebanese doesn't mark this distinction on the verb itself.

✓ Inta, btektob kteer — You (m) write a lot
✓ Hiyye btektob kteer — She writes a lot

The verb alone won't tell you which — you need the pronoun, a name, or context to know who's being talked about. This is normal in Lebanese, not a gap in what you've learned.

Rule 2 — Negating a verb
Add ma before the verb
bfee2 → ma bfee2
I wake up → I don't wake up / I'm not waking up

Drop ma in front of any conjugated verb — no other change needed:

ما بقرا
ma bo2ra
I don't read
ما بشتغل
ma beshte8il
I don't work
ما بروح
ma brou7
I don't go
Rule 3 — Negating a noun or adjective
Use mech instead of ma
mabsoot → mech mabsoot
happy → not happy

This lesson's mood words negate the same way — mech, never ma:

مش تعبان
mech te3bén
not tired
مش شاطر
mech chaater
not studious / smart
مش زعلان
mech za3lan
not upset
