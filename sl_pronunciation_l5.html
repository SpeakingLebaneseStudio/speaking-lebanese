Pronunciation Training

No new letter this lesson — instead, a feature that runs through nearly every common Lebanese verb and daily phrase: the doubled consonant. Three of this lesson's own words carry it, plus one borrowed from Lesson 1 and one from Lesson 4, so you can hear it working across the words you already know.

ّ

Gemination — the doubled consonant

shaddet ّ

A consonant held for two beats instead of one. In Arabic script it's marked by the shaddet diacritic (ّ); in Arabizi it's shown by doubling the letter (dd, ll, mm). It isn't a new sound — it's the same sound, just held a beat longer before you release it. Think of the double "t" in Italian "fatto," or the held "n" in English "unknown" said slowly. Learners who smooth it over sound slightly foreign even when their vocabulary is correct — hold it, and your speech immediately sounds more grounded.

Shadow Prompt — Gemination
Baddi 2ahwet — tfaddal, déymann bala sokkar.

I want a coffee — here you are, always without sugar. Listen. Repeat during the silence. Listen again and compare. You will hear the phrase twice with a 3-second gap.

Gemination is one of the clearest markers of natural Lebanese speech. Hold the doubled consonant for its full beat — it takes one extra count, no more — and your speech immediately sounds more grounded in the language.
