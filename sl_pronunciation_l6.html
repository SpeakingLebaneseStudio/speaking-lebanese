Pronunciation Training

This lesson moves past the seven core sounds into a bonus phonetic track: the difference between long and short vowels — a feature that shapes the rhythm of everyday Lebanese, drilled here with three of this lesson's own daily-routine words.

aa

Long vs. short vowels

aa / ee / oo

Long vowels are held for two beats; short vowels are cut to one. In Arabizi, long vowels are sometimes doubled (aa, ee, oo) or just implied by the spelling. English compresses all its vowels to a similar length — there's no single equivalent — but think of how "beat" holds its vowel longer than "bit." That stretch-versus-cut contrast is what gives Lebanese its rhythm.

Shadow Prompt — Long vs. Short Vowels
Kil yom ana bfee2 bakkeer, braaje3 darsi, w heik bkoun mabsoo6!

Every day I wake up early, I go back over my lesson, and that way I end up happy! Listen. Repeat during the silence. Listen again and compare. You will hear the phrase twice with a 3-second gap.

Long vowels are the rhythm of Lebanese — when you get them right, your speech has a natural musicality, the way a Lebanese person expects to hear it. Stretch the long ones, cut the short ones. That contrast is the key.
