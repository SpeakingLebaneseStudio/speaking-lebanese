Pronunciation Training

This lesson's bonus phonetic track moves from vowels to something that touches nearly every sentence you'll ever say in Lebanese: how the word "el" ("the") changes shape depending on what comes right after it.

ال

Linking & elision — the definite article

el (ال)

"El" doesn't stand alone — it links onto the word right after it, and exactly how it links depends on which letter comes next. There's no real English equivalent to this rule, but the closest parallel is how English quietly changes "a" to "an" before a vowel sound ("a house" vs. "an apple") — a small, automatic adjustment native speakers make without thinking, based purely on the sound that follows.

1 — Plain linking (before most letters)
"El" simply attaches to the next word — the l stays, but the two words are said as one unit, with no pause in between.
  • ija el walad → ija 'lwalad — "the boy arrived"
  • addey el sé3a → addey 'lsé3a — "how much is the watch"
2 — Sun letters (7uruf shamsiyyeh) — the l disappears
Before one of these, the l is not pronounced at all — instead, the next letter itself doubles.
Sun letters: t, th, d, dh, r, z, s, sh, ṣ, ḍ, ṭ, ẓ, l, n
  • esh-shams — "the sun" (not el-shams)
  • es-samak — "the fish" (not el-samak)
  • er-réjjél — "the man" (not el-réjjél)
  • en-nés — "the people" (not el-nés)
2b — Moon letters (7uruf 2amariyyeh) — the l stays
Before one of these, "el" is pronounced exactly as written, l included.
Moon letters: a, b, j, 7, 5, 3, 8, f, 2, k, m, h, w, y
  • el-2amar — "the moon"
  • el-bayt — "the house"
  • el-ktéb — "the book"
  • el-walad — "the boy"
el-2amar — the moon

Shadow Prompt — El (ال), Sun Letters & Moon Letters
lamman ija 'l walad ye23od ta7t esh-shams, sé3ita tole3 'l 2amar!

When the boy came to sit under the sun, at that moment the moon rose! Listen to the slow audio first — you'll hear "el" before each word as its own clear syllable. Then listen to the natural audio: before "walad" (a moon letter), "el" stays as 'l — plain linking. Before "shams" (a sun letter), the l disappears completely and the sh is held longer — "esh-shams," not "el-shams." Before "amar" (a moon letter), "el" is pronounced normally again. That contrast — l staying vs. l vanishing — is the whole rule. You will hear the phrase twice with a 3-second gap.

Once your ear catches this contrast, you'll start applying it automatically to every new word you learn — it becomes one of those things that just sounds right, the same way "an apple" sounds right and "a apple" doesn't.
